01 · PHILOSOPHY

Why this looks the way it does

Spottr is a utility tool, not an entertainment app. The aesthetic earns its keep by being honest, fast, and out of the way. Three commitments drive every visual decision.

01
Dark-first surface
OLED-black is the canonical view. Reduces driver distraction at night, preserves battery, gives the accent maximum visual weight. Light mode is a courtesy, not a parallel design.
02
Single accent discipline
One color carries meaning across the entire app: a fresh observation, an open spot, a confirmed action. Multiple accents would dilute the signal. The user learns one rule and trusts it everywhere.
03
Honest by sentence
Freshness is communicated through plain language, not colored pills. "3 min ago" and "typical for now" tell the truth about confidence without asking the user to learn a state machine.

SCREEN 07

Approach view

The screen that doesn't exist in any other parking app. Fires automatically when GPS detects the user has crossed the lot polygon. Apple Maps says "you have arrived"; Spottr takes over with this.

Live satellite tile of the actual Costco lot. User's pulsing dot enters at the bottom-left. Best zone outlined in accent with its open count. An animated dashed path traces the recommended route.

One short instruction the driver can read at a glance. No turn-by-turn voice; lots are too small. The visual works at lot speed.

  • PATHServer-computed from entry point to zone centroid via OSM aisles.
  • INSTRUCTIONServer-generated short sentence. Template: [direction], [landmark cue]. Max 8 words.

08 · THE END GOAL

The path to specific-spot routing

Spottr's end-state promise is "spot 14 is open, take me to it." Today's MVP delivers zone-level routing — the cluster of open spots and a visual approach guide. This section explains the gap, the technical constraints behind it, and the architecture that closes it over time.

The three constraints between today and spot-level

CONSTRAINT 01
Satellite resolution
Mapbox satellite tiles at maximum zoom resolve to ~30cm/pixel. A car overhead is 16×6 pixels — enough to detect "occupied or not," not enough for sub-stall certainty. Higher-resolution providers cost orders of magnitude more.
CONSTRAINT 02
Map routing snap
Apple Maps and Google Maps both refuse to route to coordinates inside a parking lot — they snap to the nearest drivable road. Pass them the lat/lng of Spot A-14 and they route to the lot entrance instead.
CONSTRAINT 03
GPS error
Consumer GPS accuracy is 5–10 meters in good conditions. A parking stall is 2.7 meters wide. Even with perfect knowledge of the right stall, the phone can't tell the user which of three adjacent stalls is correct without an additional signal.

The layered architecture that closes the gap

Each layer adds a data source. Each tightens the precision Spottr can promise. The MVP ships on Layer 1 today; BestTime layers on within weeks; everything else is a planned upgrade with a concrete vendor and cost profile.

Why the UI doesn't change as the data tightens

The interaction model is locked. The big number on the lot detail screen always says how many spots are open. The Take Me There button always opens system maps. The Approach screen always shows where to head once you arrive. What changes between Layer 1 and Layer 5 is what "where to head" means — a zone today, a row in V6.5, a specific stall in V7-C. The user learns Spottr once at MVP and grows into the precision as the architecture catches up. No app redesign. No "Spottr Pro." Just a product that gets quietly more precise.

10 · API CONTRACT

What each screen needs from the backend

Every screen has a clear data contract. Backend returns pre-formatted strings where the mobile client would otherwise have to know business rules — freshness labels, zone descriptions, approach instructions.

SCREENENDPOINTRETURNS
HomeGET /api/lots/recentArray of {lot_id, name, address, distance_mi, count, count_label, freshness_label, freshness_state}. Last 3 searched in recency order.
SearchGET /api/lots/search?q=Google Places-backed autocomplete + Spottr's known-lot enrichment. Returns {place_id, name, address, lat, lng, has_data}.
LotDetailGET /api/lots/:idFull lot record: count, freshness, capacity, current %, typical %, zone thumbnail tile URL, best entrance coord, BestTime payload.
ApproachGET /api/lots/:id/approachSatellite tile URL, GPS entry point, zone centroid, animated path geometry, instruction string. Generated server-side.
ParkedPOST /api/lots/:id/parkedSaves {user_id, lot_id, lat, lng, timestamp, zone_id}. Returns {human_readable_location, save_id}.
